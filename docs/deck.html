Ration Bolo

A voice-first ration shop assistant. Ration cardholders check stock, hear the live queue, book a time slot and report short rations by speaking — on a phone call or a WhatsApp voice note.

No app. No reading. No typing.

Sandeep Sakilam · github.com/sandeep-sakilam/ration-bolo

1

Problem: hours in a queue for grain that may not be there

  • About 80 crore people collect subsidised grain every month from about 5.5 lakh ration shops under the National Food Security Act.
  • Families arrive early and wait for hours. Many are told "stock hasn't come" or "machine is down, come tomorrow" — a wasted trip and a lost day's wage.
  • Nobody can check stock or the queue before leaving home.
  • Short rations (15 kg instead of 24 kg) go unreported: complaining means another trip, a form, or a confrontation.

Who suffers most

  • Daily-wage workers who lose a day's pay per wasted trip
  • Elderly and disabled cardholders who cannot stand for hours
  • Women who juggle the queue with work and children
  • People who cannot read or type, or own only a basic phone — the ones existing apps leave out
Ration Bolo2

Solution: one call or one voice note

  • Check stock and the family's entitlement
  • Hear the live queue and the expected wait
  • Book a 20-minute slot, confirmed by SMS or WhatsApp, with a reminder call 20 minutes before
  • Report short rations by voice and get a complaint ID
  • Dealers update stock and machine status by voice; every caller hears it first

Works on any phone: a phone call on a basic feature phone, WhatsApp voice notes on a smartphone, a web dashboard for dealers.

A real call, in Telugu

App: Namaskaram, Ration Bolo. Card chivari ankelu 0421, sare na? (card ending 0421, right?)

Lakshmi: Avunu. Maa shop ki biyyam vachaya? (yes — has rice come?)

App: Avunu, biyyam vachindi. Mee kutumbaniki 24 kilolu. (yes, 24 kg for your family)

Lakshmi: Ippudu entha mandi unnaru? (how many now?)

App: 18 mandi mundu unnaru, sumaru 36 nimishalu. (18 ahead, about 36 minutes)

Lakshmi: Repu poddhuna book cheyyandi. (book tomorrow morning)

App: Repu 10:20 ki, sare na? … Booking ayindi. Mee token B-036.

Ration Bolo4

Why voice — voice is the whole product

  • Low literacy: many cardholders cannot read an app or type a message. Everyone can speak.
  • Basic phones: a phone call works on any feature phone, with no data plan and no install.
  • Mixed languages: people mix Telugu, Hindi, Urdu and English in one sentence; we match keywords across all of them at once.
  • Speed: one sentence replaces a menu tree.

Every task can be finished by speaking

Keypad, numbered menus and buttons exist only as fallbacks.

Confirm before saving

Card digits, kilos and times are read back; nothing is saved without a "yes".

Private by default

Names are never spoken. Cards are confirmed by their last digits, phones are masked, caller audio is deleted after transcription.

Ration Bolo5

How it works

Architecture in words

  • Three channels — Twilio Voice calls, Twilio WhatsApp voice notes, and a web simulator — are thin wrappers around one dialogue engine.
  • The speech pipeline converts audio with ffmpeg and sends it to Wispr Flow (primary) or Sarvam AI (backup). Speech engines only turn audio into text.
  • The dialogue engine is a deterministic state machine: keyword and fuzzy matching (rapidfuzz) decide intent, numbers, days and yes/no. No LLM makes decisions.
  • A slot and queue engine, complaints and an in-process reminder scheduler sit behind it. One FastAPI container, SQLite or PostgreSQL.
  • Languages, phrases, keywords and entitlements live in a per-state JSON "state pack".

Slot rules

  • Each shop session is split into 20-minute windows (pilot: 10 families each).
  • Half of every window is kept for walk-ins. A booking saves waiting time; it never gives more rice or a better claim on stock.
  • One booking per card per month; the token is tied to the card.
  • More than 10 minutes late → the family joins the walk-in line.
  • The dealer serves booked and walk-in families alternately.
  • Wait estimate = people ahead × 2 minutes (30 families an hour).
  • Out of stock or machine down → new bookings pause, callers hear it first, existing bookings are told in their reminder.

Ration Bolo9

Measuring impact

Pilot targets (Round 1)

  • Booked families served within 20 minutes of their slot
  • 30% of families using a booking by month 3
  • Half as many wasted trips
  • 85% of calls completed by voice alone
  • Every complaint resolved within 7 days

Built-in metrics page tracks them

  • Average wait with and without a booking; share served within 20 minutes of the slot
  • Adoption: share of families served who had booked
  • Stock and queue checks by phone (trips not wasted); late and no-show bookings
  • Voice success rate (calls that never needed the menu), turns understood, backup-engine rate, usage per speech engine
  • Complaints filed, open, overdue and resolved within 7 days

Every turn logs which engine produced the text, so fallback rates are measured, not guessed.

Go-to-market plan

  • Pilot: one ward in Hyderabad with the Telangana Civil Supplies Department, 10 shops, 3 months.
  • Dealer onboarding: a 15-minute session per dealer; the dashboard works on any phone browser; status updates by voice.
  • Awareness at the shop: posters with the number at every pilot shop, dealer word of mouth, self-help-group (SHG) meetings and ward volunteers.
  • Telephony: move from the Twilio pilot number to an Indian toll-free number with an Indian provider (KYC).
  • Data: connect the department's ePoS feed and card database through the state-data interface.
  • Scale: add states by adding state packs (languages, phrases, entitlements) — no code changes; phrases checked by native speakers.

Milestones

  • Month 0: department agreement, 10 dealers trained, posters up
  • Month 1: toll-free number live; metrics reviewed weekly with the department
  • Month 3: 30% booking adoption, 85% voice success; decide ward-wide rollout
  • Month 6: city-wide in Hyderabad; second state pack

Works without a partner: three levels

No product with real-world dependencies is fail-proof. Ration Bolo delivers value at a shop where neither the department nor the dealer cooperates, and gets better as each one joins. A level only adds sources; it never removes the Level 0 ones, and every spoken status names its source and age.

Level 0 — nobody cooperates

  • Cardholders report to each other by voice ("rice arrived", "20 people here", "I'm at the shop", or a free missed call); confirmed when 2 phones agree, fading with age
  • Public PDS data: the state's public shop-wise sales (NFSA Section 27) shows when selling started and how fast
  • Best time to go instead of a booking, from each shop's hourly crowd profile
  • A nearby shop under One Nation One Ration Card when this one is out, down or crowded
  • Complaints reach official channels: helpline handoff, a prepared complaint, 7- and 21-day escalation
  • Public transparency page and monthly report for vigilance committees and social audits

Level 1 — a willing dealer or volunteer

  • Time-slot tokens and bookings, half of every window kept for walk-ins
  • Dealer status by one phone call or voice, cross-checked against cardholder reports
  • "Rice arrived" broadcast to opted-in cardholders, and a "Fair shop" badge callers hear
  • Where the dealer won't, a self-help-group or ward volunteer can run the tokens

Level 2 — the department joins

  • ePoS feed for stock and every sale (simulated today)
  • Card lookups and phone links from department data
  • Complaints and shop metrics routed to officials who can act

Demo: Shop 108 is Level 2; Shop 112 Level 0; Shop 115 Level 1 (dealer); Shop 109 Level 1 through a volunteer.

Ration Bolo12

Risks and limits, stated honestly

  • Government data: accurate queues and card lookups need ePoS data and phone-to-card links from each state; the prototype simulates both and labels it "Demo data".
  • Not yet toll-free: the pilot uses a Twilio US number (an international call for Indian callers); a real toll-free number needs an Indian provider with KYC.
  • SMS to India from a foreign number may not be delivered without DLT registration; confirmations go on WhatsApp where possible.
  • Speech accuracy for elderly callers, strong dialects and noisy shops is unproven until piloted; the fallback chain and the voice-success metric exist because of that.
  • Dealer resistance: dealers who benefit from crowded, unrecorded queues may resist; Level 0 works without them, and department backing helps.
  • Wispr Flow API access and limits to be confirmed with the organisers; Sarvam covers speech if Wispr is unavailable.
  • Level 0 is information, not enforcement: without a dealer or volunteer nobody can guarantee a slot; the app gives the best time to go and a nearby shop instead, and says so.
  • Cardholder reports can be wrong or gamed: agreement, decay, outlier rejection and reliability weights reduce this but can't remove it; every spoken status names its source and age.
  • Public PDS data formats differ by state and can change or go offline; the adapter is configurable and fails safely, and each state's format must be confirmed.
  • Portability depends on the state's ePoS and the cardholder's eligibility; suggestions say "usually" and never promise the other shop will serve them.
  • Complaints still depend on officials acting: the app gets them to the right channel, tracks and escalates them, and makes patterns public; it can't force a resolution.
